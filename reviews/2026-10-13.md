# Proactive Agentic Orchestration

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13485v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The authors propose POrchestra to let sub-agents request changes to delegated goals and resources during execution while preserving useful local history. (source blocks: H0037, H0046, C0002)
- The authors propose OPOD to turn attributable execution feedback into hindsight hints for self-distillation, combined with outcome supervision. (source blocks: H0057, H0365, H0357)
- The paper argues that timely revision improves benchmark success and inference efficiency, but its empirical support is specific to the evaluated systems, task subsets, and reported point estimates. (source blocks: H0071, H0072, H0091, T0047, H0373, H0184)

## Methods

- POrchestra interleaves environment actions with structured status, request, and evidence messages; the orchestrator can revise an active worker and record the update in its local history. (source blocks: H0037, H0046, C0002)
- Implemented OPOD uses a frozen base Qwen3.5-9B model for attribution hints and a frozen framework-specific SFT teacher, updating only the student through reverse-KL distillation and successful-decision cross-entropy. (source blocks: H0365, H0357)
- OPD resamples current-student actions at fixed contexts collected by the SFT policy; it is on-policy at the sampled-action level rather than a continuously refreshed environment-state distribution. (source blocks: H0357, H0365)
- Evaluation measures task success on 165 GAIA validation tasks, 128 GAIA2 mini scenarios, and a fixed 100-issue SWE-Bench Verified subset; GAIA averages are task-weighted and GAIA2 categories contain 32 scenarios each. (source blocks: T0047, H0336, H0347)
- Inference comparisons hold the orchestrator at Gemini-3-Flash while varying three execution backbones, whereas orchestrator optimization evaluates Qwen3.5-9B with DeepSeek-V4-Flash execution on GAIA and GAIA2. (source blocks: H0087, H0365)
- Mechanism analyses compare proactive communication with lifecycle/error triggers and periodic polling, including a controlled tool-revocation experiment; total execution-step budgets are shared with baselines. (source blocks: H0098, H0371)

## Author-reported results

- Table 1 reports POrchestra GAIA/GAIA2/SWE-Bench success of 71.5%/47.7%/71.0% with DeepSeek-V3.2 execution, 78.2%/53.9%/77.0% with Gemini-3-Flash execution, and 77.6%/55.5%/79.0% with DeepSeek-V4-Flash execution, the highest reported aggregate score in each benchmark–backbone comparison. (source blocks: H0071, H0072, T0047, H0087, H0373)
- For Qwen3.5-9B orchestration with DeepSeek-V4-Flash execution, OPOD reports 77.9% success on GAIA and 49.2% on GAIA2, compared with POrchestra SFT initialization at 72.4% and 43.8%, gains of 5.5 and 5.4 percentage points. (source blocks: H0091, H0087, H0336, H0347, H0373)
- Removing RWR yields GAIA/GAIA2 success of 75.8%/46.1%, and removing OPD yields 76.1%/46.1%; these point estimates favor joint supervision but do not establish a statistically significant interaction. (source blocks: H0091, H0373)
- In the controlled tool-revocation cases, proactive feedback reports 92.0% recovery, 1.67-step mean delay including idle steps, and 0.09 CA per case, versus polling every five steps at 86.0%, 2.11 steps, and 0.23 CA; the separate end-to-end GAIA2 overall scores are 55.5% and 45.3%. (source blocks: H0098)
- With Gemini-3-Flash orchestration and DeepSeek-V3.2 execution, Table 3 reports POrchestra/AOrchestra/Orchestra-o1 costs of 0.557/0.625/0.891 CA per GAIA task and 0.721/1.209/1.120 CA per GAIA2 task, including estimated GAIA communication overhead. (source blocks: T0009, H0184)
- GAIA2 revision ablations reduce overall success from 55.5% to 45.3% without task revision, 47.7% without context revision, 48.4% without tool revision, and 49.2% without budget revision; the inspected communication ablation also shows lower scores after removing each message component. (source blocks: H0176, H0347)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly limit optimization to the orchestrator, leaving sub-agent parameters and communication rules fixed; learned communication transfer across models and tasks is future work. (source blocks: H0384)
- The authors use one active sub-agent at a time and acknowledge that coordinating concurrent revision requests could provide efficiency gains for independent subtasks. (source blocks: H0385)
- The evaluation supports conclusions about the specified benchmark subsets, not the entire SWE-Bench Verified split or unrestricted deployment; GAIA also uses an LLM semantic-equivalence fallback. (source blocks: T0047, H0336, H0347)
- Selective one- or two-run evaluation and absent reported uncertainty intervals leave the robustness of small margins unresolved; controlled-intervention sample size and variability are also unspecified. (source blocks: H0373, H0091, H0098)
- Efficiency evidence concerns reported inference API costs, includes an estimated GAIA communication component, and does not establish training-inclusive economics, wall-clock efficiency, or cost portability across providers. (source blocks: T0009, H0184, H0101)
- The repository is linked but unverified here, and the authors themselves state that external deployment still requires safety, permission-handling, and privacy evaluation. (source blocks: H0112, H0387)

## Critical interpretation

- The framework is a useful operational contribution, but the mechanism claim should separate adaptive intervention timing from worker-history preservation, tool/context selection, and benchmark-specific prompting; the GAIA2 prompt additionally mandates notification handoff, so this setting is not wholly unconstrained learned proactivity. (source blocks: H0037, H0377, H0176, H0098)
- Main-text descriptions of current-policy trajectories and a same-parameter self-teacher should be aligned with the implementation’s fixed SFT-collected contexts and frozen SFT teacher, since these distinctions affect the meaning of on-policy optimization and possible distribution shift. (source blocks: H0357, H0365)
- The headline small-orchestrator aggregate improvement lacks a specified comparator and averaging rule; benchmark-specific gains and within-framework ablations are interpretable, but the headline aggregate should not be treated as independently verified. (source blocks: H0004, H0091)
- Proposition 1 formalizes delay under uniform arrivals and a fixed lifecycle boundary rather than guaranteeing successful proactive detection, while Proposition 2 establishes normalized-update error reduction under untested noise assumptions; shorter variance upper bounds do not prove smaller actual attribution variance or higher task success. (source blocks: H0224, H0264, H0285)
- The length analysis is outcome-selected and method-dependent, and its inspected curves contain exceptions; bin counts, uncertainty, and independently defined task-length strata are needed before concluding that gains reliably increase with horizon. (source blocks: H0105, H0099)
- The attribution prompt sensibly restricts hints to decision-time-feasible corrections, but illustrative hints—including those from failed cases—do not validate causal attribution accuracy or successful counterfactual repair; similarly, the cost frontier is descriptive of displayed systems rather than a general Pareto-optimality guarantee. (source blocks: H0057, H0377, H0190, H0101)
