# Judging Before Propagation: Task-Graph Contextual Integrity for Multi-Agent Privacy

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39532v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The paper proposes Task-Graph Contextual Integrity as a graph-level conjunction of history-conditioned atomic CI judgments over an item's intended propagation scope, rather than a new standalone definition of atomic appropriateness. (source blocks: H0064, H0038)
- Full-Scope rejects a release when any anticipated downstream transfer is inadmissible, whereas Auto-Region admits an item into its rooted admissible region and removes it at prohibited propagation frontiers. (source blocks: H0073, H0075, H0079)
- MAPLE-Bench offers controlled evaluation of contextual disclosure policies and cumulative fact-pair violations in synthetic multi-agent tasks, but its topology variants reuse underlying workflows rather than supplying independent task populations. (source blocks: H0085, T0018, T0019)
- The authors' experiments support a descriptive privacy–cost–utility trade-off in the evaluated configurations, not universal privacy protection, unconditional secrecy from compromised agents, or a proof that local checking must fail. (source blocks: H0090, H0101, T0016, H0028, H0032)

## Methods

- The implementation evaluates memory items sequentially using a transaction-local conservative disclosure state that includes delivered and already authorized information, committing tentative state changes only for an authorized response. (source blocks: H0069, H0158)
- Propagation authorization is computed on a fixed task graph; Full-Scope checks the complete downstream closure, while Auto-Region stops expansion at inadmissible edges and installs item-specific frontier rules. (source blocks: H0067, H0073, H0075, H0079)
- Frontier filtering combines lexical matching, MiniLM cosine screening at 0.35, and DeBERTa bidirectional entailment with a 0.70 flagging threshold; the 0.75 similarity threshold marks audit risk rather than bypassing entailment. (source blocks: H0197, H0203)
- The benchmark comprises 48 workflows instantiated under three topologies to yield 144 cases across four domains, including 108 one-attacker and 36 two-attacker cases; each case contains four executing agents, a terminal recipient, 30 memory items, and five multiple-choice questions. (source blocks: H0085, T0018, T0019)
- The authors compare No Protection, Local-History CI, Online Atomic CI, and the two TG-CI modes under Haiku–Luna, Haiku–Haiku, and Luna–Luna task-agent/privacy-judge assignments, using Local-History CI as an in-framework comparator rather than an exact reproduction of another system. (source blocks: H0090, H0092)
- Evaluation distinguishes repeated unauthorized delivery events from deduplicated inappropriate item–agent exposures, measures utility by five-question accuracy, and counts privacy-judge LLM tokens while excluding local filtering inference. (source blocks: H0094, H0109)

## Author-reported results

- For Haiku–Luna, the authors report mean unauthorized delivery-event counts averaged across the four domains of 15.84 for No Protection, 10.47 for Local-History CI, 6.11 for Online Atomic CI, 1.49 for Auto-Region, and 0.62 for Full-Scope; the inspected domain panels support these rounded means, without reported confidence intervals. (source blocks: H0092, H0094, H0101)
- In the same configuration, reported mean privacy-judge usage is 133.1 thousand tokens for Auto-Region and 144.5 thousand for Full-Scope, excluding local MiniLM/DeBERTa computation; this is judge-token accounting, not a measurement of total runtime, monetary cost, or total system compute. (source blocks: H0092, H0094, H0101)
- For Haiku–Luna, reported four-domain mean inappropriate item–agent exposure counts are 2.90 for No Protection, 1.58 for Local-History CI, 0.99 for Online Atomic CI, 0.35 for Auto-Region, and 0.16 for Full-Scope, with repeated delivery of an item to the same compromised agent counted only once. (source blocks: H0094, H0106)
- Table 2 reports Haiku–Luna accuracies of 83.26% for Local-History CI, 80.00% for Auto-Region, and 77.92% for Full-Scope, corresponding to 96.08% and 93.58% retention of Local-History accuracy; under Haiku–Haiku the corresponding accuracies are 78.06%, 68.40%, and 62.22%, and under Luna–Luna they are 96.11%, 92.22%, and 88.06%, without uncertainty intervals. (source blocks: T0016, H0109)
- The inspected main and appendix topology plots place both TG-CI modes below the three baselines at every displayed domain/topology combination, and the appendix exposure plots also favor TG-CI, but these are descriptive results for the evaluated small fixed graphs rather than statistical evidence of topology invariance. (source blocks: H0118, C0022, C0027, C0028, C0033, C0034, C0039)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly assume mediated memory and messaging interfaces, exclude out-of-band communication and covert encodings, and do not seek secrecy from compromised agents whose access is contextually authorized. (source blocks: H0028, H0032)
- The authors leave dynamic replanning to future work, and their conservative-state argument additionally requires downward-closed admissibility and an authorization footprint that remains an upper bound at delivery. (source blocks: H0067, H0158)
- The authors acknowledge that arbitrary intermediate semantic transformations are outside the core formalism; information-increasing combinations require separate state updates or judgments whose comprehensive runtime enforcement is not established in the inspected account. (source blocks: H0205, H0211, H0079)
- The benchmark uses synthetic data, limited graph sizes, reused workflow templates, and task-specific privacy norms that the authors explicitly decline to treat as universal, restricting conclusions about real deployment and broad task generalization. (source blocks: H0085, T0018, T0019, H0124)
- Reviewer-identified reporting uncertainties include an unspecified repeated-run and seed protocol, absent confidence intervals, unreported standalone gate precision/recall and threshold sensitivity, and supplementary code/data that were not inspected here. (source blocks: H0101, T0016, H0126, H0197, H0203)
- Appendix Figures 6 and 7 call their right-axis metric extra execution tokens relative to No Protection, whereas their inspected legends identify CI-judge input and output tokens; the manuscript does not reconcile these labels, so no total-execution-cost conclusion is retained. (source blocks: H0094, C0022, C0027)

## Critical interpretation

- The graph-scoped authorization and conservative state tracking form a coherent mechanism, but the empirical comparison does not isolate prospective reasoning from topology blocking, semantic frontier filtering, or implementation-specific judge errors; an ideal sound atomic check at every realized transfer would not permit an inadmissible transfer merely because it lacks lookahead. (source blocks: H0064, H0069, H0079, H0090, H0101)
- The safety argument is conditional rather than an end-to-end security proof: the paper should establish how concurrent releases, newly derived facts, and stale authorizations preserve its delivery-time state upper bound, and whether its LLM judge respects the required monotonicity. (source blocks: H0158, H0205, H0211)
- Recipient-specific histories and deduplicated item–agent exposure do not establish resistance to coalition-level inference from separately authorized observations, so the discussion of limiting information available to a compromised coalition is broader than the demonstrated measurement. (source blocks: H0106, H0028, H0032, H0038)
- Unauthorized event counts can fall because agents receive or transmit less information or take different trajectories, not only because decisions become more accurate; reporting violation rates per opportunity, authorized-information recall, and trajectory changes alongside accuracy would distinguish these explanations. (source blocks: H0073, H0079, H0094, H0101, T0016)
- Reusing tasks across topologies is useful for paired structural comparisons, but statistical uncertainty should be clustered by underlying workflow rather than treating all case or question instances as independent; the existing plots support descriptive consistency, not a general robustness claim. (source blocks: T0018, T0019, H0118, C0033, C0039)
- The much lower utility retention with a Haiku judge makes the privacy–utility conclusion model-dependent, while exclusion of local filtering costs and inconsistent appendix axis descriptions preclude a complete efficiency comparison; judge calibration, total latency, and local inference costs should accompany the current token results. (source blocks: H0092, H0094, T0016, H0197, H0203, C0022, C0027)
