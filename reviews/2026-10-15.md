# BRIDGE: Selective Boundary Repair with Dependency Graph Evidence in Multi-Agent Systems

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17805v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The authors propose BRIDGE as a selective handoff controller that combines public dependency-graph evidence, offline intervention calibration and verification before releasing a modified message. (source blocks: C0001, C0002, H0032, H0062)
- The central scientific hypothesis is that repairing every observable communication gap is inferior to selecting repairs by action–frontier utility; the reported ablations are consistent with this hypothesis but do not establish universal component necessity. (source blocks: T0014, T0015, T0011, H0186)
- BRIDGE's claimed verification guarantee concerns declared local graph postconditions and unreleased messages, not guaranteed task completion, semantic correctness of every fact or recovery of opaque private agent state. (source blocks: H0062, H0103, H0225)

## Methods

- The controller represents messages, observations and obligations as graph nodes and tracks production, routing and consumption edges to identify unconsumed delivery, missing evidence and routing-mismatch frontiers; candidate primitives include ASK, INTEGRATE, VERIFY, DECOMPOSE and RETRIEVE. (source blocks: H0032, H0033, T0002)
- HandoffProbe branches a shared faulted handoff prefix into native and repaired continuations, runs both to termination, and uses deterministic binary task acceptance to label helped, harmed or unchanged outcomes. (source blocks: C0001, H0048, H0046, H0193, H0195)
- The reported calibration corpus contains 200 episodes split 160/20/20; the base utility weights helped outcomes by 1.0 and subtracts 0.25 times neutrality, 2.0 times harm and 0.25 times normalized cost, with admission requiring positive utility, empirical harm at most 0.05 and support of at least five samples. (source blocks: H0165, T0014, T0015)
- Runtime execution stages a candidate before release and restores the local graph and native message on failed verification; the paper's deterministic-processing description is qualified by conflicting token-bearing diagnosis and INTEGRATE descriptions in Appendix F. (source blocks: H0062, T0023, H0241, H0235)
- Evaluation uses deterministic benchmark acceptance, 90 TeamBench tasks per backbone and 90 SILO instances with one DeepSeek rollout or three GPT rollouts per task; TeamBench priors and thresholds are reported as frozen from synthetic calibration, and reference methods are handoff-level adaptations using the same backbone. (source blocks: T0019, H0193, H0195, H0199, H0203)

## Author-reported results

- On SILO-BENCH, Table 2 reports DeepSeek full success of 63.3±3.3% for Native and 75.6±3.8% for BRIDGE, and GPT full success of 68.9±4.0% and 77.8±1.1%; Appendix D calls the uncertainties standard errors, with 90 DeepSeek evaluations and 270 GPT task-seed evaluations over 90 instances. (source blocks: T0009, H0197, T0019)
- On the 90-task TeamBench subset, the authors report Native-to-BRIDGE full-pass changes of 48.9% to 60.0% for DeepSeek and 46.7% to 52.2% for GPT, without tabled uncertainty; the DeepSeek paired-outcome table is internally inconsistent with its reported pass-rate change. (source blocks: T0009, T0019, T0021)
- For SILO-BENCH, the authors report 16 BRIDGE-only wins versus five Native-only wins for DeepSeek with p=0.027, and 36 versus 12 for pooled GPT with p&lt;0.001; these are reported paired results, not independently reproduced significance tests. (source blocks: T0021, T0019)
- On a curated MAST diagnostic set of 281 positive traces and 281 controls, BRIDGE is reported to achieve 68.6% F1 at 9,116 tokens per trace for DeepSeek and 69.8% at 8,664 for GPT; DeepSeek MOC scores 68.7%, GPT VeriMAP scores 69.4%, and Direct Prompting uses fewer tokens than BRIDGE on both backbones. (source blocks: T0010, T0017, H0177)
- The DeepSeek SILO ablation table reports 75.6% full-system success, 68.9% with a flat checklist, 70.0% with an LLM self-gate, and 56.7% after removing either utility gating or local verification; these are reference-seed results without tabled uncertainty, and the appendix's stated drop magnitude conflicts with its endpoints. (source blocks: H0197, T0011, H0186)
- Table 14 reports SILO cumulative footprints of 30,352 versus 25,198 Native tokens per GPT task and 33,791 versus 22,460 per DeepSeek task, labeled +20.4% and +50.5%; its separate official-agent-margin row reports +2.0% and +24.5%, which must not be presented as reconciled end-to-end overhead. (source blocks: T0023, H0074)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly limit applicability to handoff failures observable in public execution records and state that empirical utilities require backbone-specific calibration; the tested cross-environment transfer therefore does not establish general transfer across models or opaque workflows. (source blocks: H0103, H0199)
- Small action–frontier calibration segments and an empirical harm threshold provide no demonstrated statistical guarantee on deployment harm, and binary terminal-outcome neutrality can conceal intermediate costs or disruptions. (source blocks: H0048, H0165, T0015, T0016)
- The curated, framework-imbalanced MAST sample is evidence about trace diagnosis rather than runtime repair effectiveness; the inspected diagnostic tables do not establish precision, recall, specificity, F1 averaging convention or uncertainty. (source blocks: T0010, T0017, H0177, H0181)
- The inspected paper does not adequately specify the contextual or historical utility adjustment, or the denominator and uncertainty behind the claimed 87.5% unchanged unselected repairs; these remain author-reported observations rather than fully auditable quantitative findings. (source blocks: T0011, H0099, H0095)
- Token-accounting discrepancies, inconsistent TeamBench paired outcomes and conflicting GPT boundary counts remain unresolved source-side defects; supplementary implementations and manifests are claimed by the authors but were not inspected or independently replicated here. (source blocks: T0021, T0023, H0107, H0233, H0243)

## Critical interpretation

- Separating feasibility, empirical utility and local verification is a useful design, and the verified-but-failed TeamBench example appropriately exposes its scope; however, the main method's zero-LLM INTEGRATE description conflicts with Appendix F's 41 prompted reconstructions, while nonzero diagnosis charges require an explicit distinction between graph maintenance and semantic diagnosis. (source blocks: H0062, T0023, H0241, H0235, H0225)
- The headline efficiency interpretation is not supported as end-to-end accounting: Table 14's cumulative overheads are materially larger than its official-agent margins, and the GPT agent subtotal of 25,786 differs from its official value of 25,693 without explanation. A common accounting ledger covering all controller calls is needed before accepting claims of negligible overall overhead. (source blocks: T0023, H0074)
- The TeamBench DeepSeek paired counts imply a 10.0-percentage-point change across 90 paired tasks, whereas Table 2 reports an 11.1-point change; neither should be silently corrected or selected as authoritative. For pooled GPT SILO inference, repeated runs share task instances, so the analysis should identify the test and address task-level dependence rather than treating 270 evaluations as 270 distinct tasks. (source blocks: T0009, T0019, T0021)
- The ablations suggest useful components but do not justify the paper's categorical indispensability language: Appendix C states a 23.3-point drop for endpoints differing by 18.9 points, its reported p-values are 0.063 and 0.070, and the 73.3% configuration is labeled aggregate gating in the table but frontier-specific calibration in the prose. (source blocks: T0011, H0186, H0095)
- The gate uses transparent utility arithmetic, but admitted evidence can be as small as six episodes with no observed harms; confidence-aware admission, a specified adjustment rule and sensitivity to utility weights are needed to substantiate reliable deployment boundaries. The unsupported denominator for the 87.5% selectivity claim further limits the mechanism-level explanation. (source blocks: T0014, T0015, T0016, H0099, H0095)
- Diagnostic efficiency should be comparator-specific: BRIDGE is cheaper than the adapted multistage methods but more expensive than Direct Prompting, and the claimed 32%–42% savings do not hold for every adapted comparator. Small reported F1 differences without uncertainty, uneven framework representation and incompletely specified diagnostic adaptations support a measured comparison, not a general superiority claim. (source blocks: T0010, T0017, H0177, H0181, H0203)
