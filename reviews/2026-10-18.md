# SSR: Structural Signal Retriever for Scalable Agent Discovery in LLM-Orchestrated Multi-Agent Systems

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21113v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The authors formulate candidate-context scalability as separating registry-wide retrieval from the small candidate set presented to an LLM, while explicitly acknowledging that pre-selection, IDF weighting, and inverted indexing are established mechanisms. (source blocks: H0011, H0057, H0189)
- SSR is a deterministic, training-free lexical retrieval design whose structural signals originate in capability-bearing Agent Card fields; structural does not mean learned semantic or syntactic structure. (source blocks: H0011, H0060, H0061, T0006)
- The evidence supports compact-candidate orchestration as a general architectural benefit rather than an SSR-exclusive advantage, and does not establish SSR as a generally stronger retriever than BM25. (source blocks: H0129, T0025, T0028, H0202)
- SSR's explicit signal contributions provide retrieval-score traceability, but neither that property nor the pipeline schematic establishes explainability of LLM arbitration or successful downstream agent execution. (source blocks: H0060, H0198, C0001, H0138)

## Methods

- SSR lowercases text, extracts \[a-z0-9\]+ units, removes short tokens and fixed stopwords, deduplicates unigram/bigram signals, retains at most 64 signals per query or card, and scores overlap by summing log((N+1)/(df+1))+1 weights. (source blocks: H0061, H0062, T0006, T0007)
- The index retrieves the union of postings for query signals, excluding only zero-score cards; candidates are ordered by score, matched-signal count, and identifier, then truncated to at most K without padding, including an empty result when no signals match. (source blocks: H0074, H0057, H0075, T0014)
- RQ1 evaluates all 250 synthetic benchmark queries in target-preserving nested registries of 10, 100, 500, and 1,000 cards, comparing full catalog with SSR at K=10 using identical index/name/description projections and deterministically alternating execution order. (source blocks: H0095, H0106, H0107, H0101, C0003)
- RQ2 adapts 8,841,823 MS MARCO passages into document-derived cards, evaluates indexed retrieval effectiveness on 6,980 eligible queries, and uses a common seeded 200-query subset for online timing and exhaustive-score agreement; comparisons include TF-IDF, BM25, equal-weight structural overlap, and exhaustive Structural+IDF. (source blocks: H0109, H0121, T0025, H0142)
- RQ3 uses 200 queries sampled before retrieval and query-specific nested registries of 100, 500, 1,000, and 5,000 cards, comparing full catalog, SSR, and BM25 with matched shortlist budgets and arbitration; K=20 received retrospective retrieval-only corroboration on 1,000 disjoint queries using a within-0.02 recall rule. (source blocks: H0126, H0129, H0134, H0136, H0127)
- LLM arbitration uses gpt-4o-mini at temperature zero with validated JSON indices; end-to-end failures include skipped empty pools and API/parser errors, accuracy uncertainty uses Wilson intervals, and SSR–BM25 correctness comparisons use exact paired McNemar tests with Holm correction, while latency excludes offline indexing and failed requests. (source blocks: H0138, H0139, H0141, H0149)

## Author-reported results

- On the 250-query synthetic RQ1 benchmark at 1,000 cards, the authors report mean input falling from 63,645 to 767 tokens and mean successful online latency from 2.647 to 0.769 seconds for full catalog versus SSR, corresponding to 98.8% and 70.9% reductions; estimated per-query inference cost falls from $0.009551 to $0.000119, without reported uncertainty intervals. (source blocks: T0024, H0101, H0107, H0141)
- For indexed effectiveness on 6,980 MS MARCO queries, adding IDF to structural overlap raises reported R@10 from 0.3209 to 0.3378 and MRR@10 from 0.1462 to 0.1544, but BM25 remains stronger at 0.3890 and 0.1829, respectively; these are descriptive point estimates without reported uncertainty for these differences. (source blocks: H0109, H0121, T0025)
- On the common 200-query RQ2 timing/agreement subset, the authors report exact unpadded top-10 agreement with exhaustive Structural+IDF, while mean explicitly scored passages decline from 8,841,823 to 286,638 and mean retrieval latency from 69,848.3 to 941.5 milliseconds, yielding rounded reductions of 96.8% in explicit scoring and a 74.2-fold speedup over exhaustive scoring, not over BM25. (source blocks: H0121, T0025, H0163)
- At 5,000-card RQ3 registries, reported candidate recall is 0.950 for SSR versus 0.970 for BM25, conditional selection accuracy is 0.9316 versus 0.9227, and end-to-end accuracy over all 200 query–registry pairs is 0.885 versus 0.895, with marginal 95% Wilson intervals \[0.833,0.922\] and \[0.845,0.930\]; the authors report Holm-adjusted end-to-end McNemar p=1.000 at every registry size, which does not demonstrate equivalence. (source blocks: H0126, H0138, H0139, T0027, H0175)
- At 5,000 cards, none of the 200 full-catalog requests succeeded, whereas every attempted SSR and BM25 request succeeded; SSR attempted every query and BM25 attempted 99.0%, with empty pools counted as end-to-end failures. Table V reports mean input of 1,690 tokens for SSR and 1,561 for BM25, versus a prompt-based full-catalog estimate of 447,761 tokens; the corresponding $0.067164 full-catalog cost is an estimate, not measured failed-request spending. (source blocks: H0129, H0138, T0028, H0179, H0141)
- The authors report that retrospective validation on 1,000 disjoint queries selected K=20 as the smallest tested budget meeting the within-0.02 recall rule at every registry size; this supports that operating point within the tested design but is not prospective held-out end-to-end validation. (source blocks: H0134, H0136, H0170, H0203)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly limit RQ2 and RQ3 to a document-derived Agent Card abstraction that lacks native skills, interfaces, authentication requirements, and operational metadata, so these experiments do not establish production agent-discovery validity. (source blocks: H0200, H0109, H0127)
- The synthetic benchmark's controlled generator, designated targets, and capability twins enable reproducible scaling but do not capture real documentation heterogeneity, access restrictions, capability evolution, or multiple acceptable agents. (source blocks: H0095, H0201, C0003)
- Explicit lexical matching is vulnerable to vocabulary mismatch, and the reported MS MARCO comparison favors BM25; IDF and indexing ablations do not establish that native field provenance independently improves retrieval. (source blocks: H0060, T0025, H0202, H0200)
- The original budget sensitivity analysis reused the end-to-end query sample, and retrospective disjoint retrieval validation does not remove that design limitation or establish a universally optimal shortlist size. (source blocks: H0134, H0136, H0170, H0203)
- Operational conclusions are restricted to one LLM/API configuration and 200 RQ3 queries; separately executed BM25 runs, successful-request-only latency, and omission of index construction, storage, and maintenance prevent broad lifecycle-cost or latency generalization. (source blocks: H0141, H0149, H0204)
- Reproducibility remains unverified here because the linked code and per-query artifacts were not inspected; the paper does not enumerate its fixed stopwords, supply the complete candidate-budget validation grid or paired discordant counts, or provide API-error diagnostics sufficient to establish the full-catalog failure mechanism. (source blocks: H0061, H0150, H0175, H0179, H0170)

## Critical interpretation

- The matched BM25 shortlist is an important control: it supports the general value of candidate pre-selection while substantially narrowing the SSR-specific empirical contribution to its explicit representation, internal ablations, and traceable scoring rather than superior effectiveness or uniquely reduced context. (source blocks: H0011, H0129, T0025, T0028, H0198)
- The mathematical pool-size guarantee is sound for candidate count, but a fixed K alone does not guarantee fixed prompt tokens when card descriptions vary in length, nor does it guarantee registry-independent retrieval work; deployment claims need both serialization-length controls and posting-distribution measurements. (source blocks: H0057, T0014, H0106, H0189)
- The largest external-validity gap is the absence of native operational Agent Cards in the public-corpus experiments; a field-preserving versus flattened-text ablation on such cards is needed to distinguish the value of Agent Card structure from ordinary sparse lexical matching. (source blocks: H0060, H0061, H0200, H0142)
- The reported paired testing appropriately avoids an equivalence claim, but a small nonsignificant comparison cannot establish interchangeability; prospective budget selection, paired effect intervals, and disclosed discordant counts would make the effectiveness comparison more informative and auditable. (source blocks: H0136, H0139, H0175, H0203)
- The strong comparison with exhaustive structural scoring demonstrates the usefulness of indexing that scorer, not a competitive retrieval-speed advantage: the same RQ2 table reports lower mean latency for BM25, and lifecycle efficiency remains unmeasured. (source blocks: T0025, H0163, H0204)
- The full-catalog failure observation should remain configuration-specific rather than being assigned an unreported API cause, and the pipeline figure's delegation and aggregation stages should not be mistaken for evaluated task completion, since effectiveness is defined by selecting a relevant card. (source blocks: H0138, H0179, H0204, C0001)
