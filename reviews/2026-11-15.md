# From Delegation to Coordination: Learning Efficient Multi-Agent Deep Research

https://arxiv.org/html/2611.17041v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- LEAD-RL addresses the mismatch between team outcomes and individual report utility by jointly training a shared main-agent/subagent policy while selectively filtering subagent advantages. (source blocks: H0009, H0010, H0174, H0081)
- The method retains final-answer correctness as the scalar team reward, but additionally uses reference-informed report judgments to determine which outcome advantages reach subagent trajectories. (source blocks: H0010, H0069, H0174, H0164)
- The reported BrowseComp comparison supports a better accuracy–critical-path trade-off than shared-credit joint RL in the authors' harness, without establishing statistical significance, minimum execution cost, or wall-clock acceleration. (source blocks: H0100, T0011, H0115, H0048, H0050)
- Improvements across both evaluated model families and all five benchmark columns support breadth of the complete pipeline's reported gains, whereas the isolated contribution-aware RL evidence is narrower and centered on BrowseComp. (source blocks: H0091, T0009, T0011, H0104)

## Methods

- A shared policy performs routing, isolated subagent research, and synthesis; role-conditioned local and global verifiers return failed reports or answers for revision without using reference answers at inference. (source blocks: H0058, H0064)
- Training uses four complete rollouts per question and group-relative final-answer advantages, with the main agent inheriting the team advantage directly and complete agent interactions serving as logical training trajectories. (source blocks: H0064, H0065, H0174, H0176)
- The separate contribution judge receives the question, reference answer, delegated brief, report, and bounded captured evidence, but neither the main agent's final answer nor its correctness verdict; low-confidence non-neutral judgments below 0.8 become neutral. (source blocks: H0069, H0072, H0170, H0166, H0164)
- Helpful reports retain only positive team advantages, harmful reports only negative advantages, and neutral reports the original signal; normalized, annealed subagent weights enter a joint GSPO objective with a KL penalty. (source blocks: H0174, H0081, H0176)
- The RL comparisons reportedly share SFT initialization, training data, validation set, question split, decoding configuration, harness, and execution budget, while the broad benchmark table also includes external results obtained under potentially different protocols. (source blocks: H0095, H0098, H0100)
- Evaluation separates accuracy from DeepSearchQA's mean per-question answer-set F1 and assesses efficiency through total tokens, main-agent steps plus eventwise longest subagent branches, and the sequential-equivalent-to-critical-path ratio for each workload. (source blocks: H0093, T0002, T0003)

## Author-reported results

- In Table 1, the Ling pipeline's reported percentages rise from 58.0 to 73.5 on BrowseComp, 71.3 to 76.3 on BrowseComp-ZH, 73.1 to 94.2 on GAIA, 30.8 to 56.2 on tool-augmented HLE, and 80.2 to 86.3 in DeepSearchQA mean answer-set F1; corresponding Qwen values rise from 58.3 to 70.0, 70.9 to 72.9, 81.6 to 88.4, 41.7 to 54.3, and 83.2 to 85.2, with no uncertainty intervals shown. (source blocks: H0091, H0093, T0009, H0104)
- On the reported full-BrowseComp training-variant comparison, LEAD-RL achieves 73.49% accuracy versus 71.00% for shared-credit joint RL, a 2.49-percentage-point gain, while mean critical-path cost falls from 323.30 to 244.22 steps per question, a rounded 24.5% reduction; no uncertainty intervals are supplied. (source blocks: H0091, T0011, H0115)
- Table 3 reports 31.52% helpful and 9.87% harmful reports for LEAD-RL versus 21.15% and 13.84% for shared-credit joint RL, with idealized speedup of 1.71× versus 1.39×; relative to SFT's 72.50% accuracy and 403.37 critical-path steps per question, LEAD-RL has higher accuracy and a rounded 39.5% lower critical-path cost, but report counts and uncertainty are not provided. (source blocks: T0011, H0116, H0048)
- In matched-budget BrowseComp runs, enabling swarm execution changes base Ling accuracy from 59.0% to 58.0% but changes trained LEAD-RL accuracy from 66.5% to 73.5%; among swarm-enabled runs, base-to-trained tokens rise from 330.7k to 336.9k per question while critical-path cost falls from 251.0 to 244.22 steps per question. (source blocks: T0010)
- The authors report a 6.78-percentage-point validation accuracy gain at approximately constant critical-path cost over the common recorded horizon; the inspected validation figure supports this qualitative trajectory, but its first point follows an RL update and its pixels do not independently establish the exact gain. (source blocks: H0119, H0120)
- The inspected on-policy plots show lower critical-path growth for LEAD-RL during the shared training horizon, followed by a late cost increase without sustained correctness improvement in its longer run; the authors explicitly distinguish these changing-batch measurements from generalization estimates. (source blocks: H0180, H0187)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly limit idealized speedup to step-level parallelism: heterogeneous tool latency, communication, contention, and redundant work prevent interpreting it as measured wall-clock or economic acceleration. (source blocks: H0048, H0050, T0003)
- The authors assess potential evidential utility rather than causal report contribution and acknowledge that reduced corrective search is an unmeasured explanation, not an established mechanism for the efficiency gains. (source blocks: H0027, H0116)
- External baselines retain differing evaluation protocols, and the broad base-to-trained results measure the complete pipeline rather than isolating the contribution-aware RL stage. (source blocks: H0091, H0095, H0104)
- Reviewer assessment: the inspected paper does not report uncertainty intervals, repeated-seed variability, report-rate counts, training-data composition, or sufficient numerical split, decoding, and execution-budget details to assess significance and reproduce the comparisons fully. (source blocks: H0176, H0091, H0098, H0100, T0009, T0010, T0011)
- Reviewer assessment: both supervision channels use the same external judge model, confidence is explicitly uncalibrated, and evidence can be truncated; no independent human agreement or truncation-sensitivity evaluation is reported, leaving label reliability uncertain. (source blocks: H0170, H0166, H0164)
- The authors caution that on-policy runs have unequal horizons and changing questions, identify late efficiency deterioration, and present only a selected qualitative case whose returned facts are not independently annotated ground truth. (source blocks: H0180, H0187, H0189, H0203)

## Critical interpretation

- The matched SFT starting point and shared harness make Table 3 more informative about the RL design than the external leaderboard comparisons, but the modest accuracy gain over SFT needs paired outcome uncertainty and independent training runs before its robustness can be judged. (source blocks: H0095, H0098, H0100, T0011)
- The reported ablations compare training packages rather than separately identifying the effects of semantic label gating, subagent normalization, and weight annealing; unequal recorded training horizons also warrant explicit compute-matched and checkpoint-selection analyses. (source blocks: H0174, H0081, H0176, H0098, T0011, H0180)
- The phrase outcome-only should be restricted to the scalar team reward: reference-informed utility labels alter the optimization signal even without being added to that reward, so the learning procedure is not informed solely by final correctness. (source blocks: H0010, H0069, H0174, H0164)
- The local/global verification loops and the contribution gate plausibly interact, but the supplied comparisons do not isolate verifier effects or test whether shared-policy verification catches errors independently rather than reinforcing correlated judgments. (source blocks: H0058, H0098, T0011, H0164)
- The evidence supports a favorable reported accuracy–critical-path trade-off, not a general claim of lower total compute or deployment cost; direct latency, resource, and training-judge accounting would be needed to connect the abstraction to practical efficiency. (source blocks: T0010, T0011, H0048, H0050, H0164)
