# Jev-LiteMAS: Separating Decisions from Generation in Multi-Agent Workflows

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21411v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The authors motivate operation-level capability allocation by distinguishing bounded expert selection and initial artifact screening from open-ended expert generation, planning instructions and corrective feedback. (source blocks: H0009, H0062, H0083)
- Jev-LiteMAS replaces a pre-generated execution trajectory with legal-action routing informed by current artifacts, while selected generative experts retain the original task, intermediate results and role instructions. (source blocks: H0062, H0069)
- The proposed review cascade lets passing artifacts bypass generative review, escalates flagged artifacts for independent acceptance or revision feedback, and sends repaired artifacts through screening again; local acceptance is distinct from workflow termination. (source blocks: H0079, H0083, C0002, H0210)
- In the authors' six profiled DeepSeek-V4-Flash configurations spanning MetaGPT, LAS and AgentNet on MATH and MBPP+, routing plus review accounts for 31.7–43.5% of generative tokens and 29.5–46.1% of recorded latency. (source blocks: H0008, C0001)

## Methods

- The implementation uses AutoGen, Jev coordination and DeepSeek-V4-Flash or GPT-5 generative backbones; Qwen3-1.7B-Instruct supplies the small-model baseline, and most component analyses use DeepSeek. (source blocks: H0105)
- All methods use fixed shared evaluation subsets: 150 category-stratified instances each from MATH, MBPP+ and IDRBench I3, sampled with seed 42, plus 150 API-Bank episodes containing 445 API-call prediction points. (source blocks: H0204)
- Evaluation uses mathematical answer equivalence for MATH, EvalPlus pass@1 requiring both base and extended tests for MBPP+, strict official-checker API invocation accuracy for API-Bank, and binary integrability-label accuracy for I3. (source blocks: H0205)
- Reported token counts cover generative requests rather than decision-model computation, while latency and generative-call counts summarize complete workflows; API-Bank accuracy is per prediction point but resource averages are per episode. (source blocks: H0206, H0105)
- The review study injects task-relevant errors into an intermediate artifact from a randomly selected expert on MATH and MBPP+, comparing workflow accuracy and resource use alongside author-labeled attack success and screening FPR/TPR. (source blocks: T0016, T0017, H0115)

## Author-reported results

- Across the eight task/backbone configurations in Table 1, the authors report 12.7–54.6% fewer generative tokens than LAS, displayed accuracy differences within 1.34 percentage points, and lower mean latency in seven configurations; GPT-5 MATH is slower at 30.04 versus 25.91 seconds. These are descriptive results on the fixed subsets, not demonstrated statistical equivalence. (source blocks: H0204, H0206, T0009, T0010)
- Table 2(b) reports Content-Blind versus Jev Router accuracies of 57.33% versus 75.33% on I3 and 57.30% versus 66.29% on API-Bank, with respective generative-call averages of 2.46 versus 2.47 and 4.13 versus 4.00; the displayed accuracy gaps are 18.00 and 8.99 points, with the latter conflicting with the introduction's 9.02-point claim. (source blocks: T0015, C0004, H0013)
- Under injected errors, Table 3 reports cascaded-workflow accuracies of 94.00% on MATH and 84.67% on MBPP+, using approximately 14.6% and 21.7% fewer generative tokens than Jev–LLM; Table 4 reports Jev screening TPRs of 95.33% and 91.33%, compared with LLM-judge TPRs of 96.67% and 92.00%, while Jev's clean-case FPRs are higher at 4.00% and 6.67% versus 1.33% and 5.33%. (source blocks: T0016, T0017, H0115)
- In Table 5(a), raising the generative-call cap from two to six increases reported I3 accuracy from 46.67% to 73.33% and API-Bank prediction accuracy from 56.18% to 65.84%, with average executed calls rising from 2.00 to 2.64 per I3 instance and from 2.00 to 4.46 per API-Bank episode. (source blocks: T0018, H0204, H0206)
- In Table 5(b), AAA/ABB/ABA backbone assignments yield I3 accuracies of 75.33/76.67/78.00% but API-Bank accuracies of 66.29/66.07/64.49%, with generative-token use increasing on both tasks; heterogeneous execution therefore has task-dependent rather than uniformly beneficial effects. (source blocks: T0018)
- For the GPT-5 workflow, Figure 3 reports Jev request-time contributions of 2.06 seconds on MATH, 2.08 on MBPP+, 1.13 per API-Bank episode and 2.21 per I3 instance, corresponding to 6.85%, 5.17%, 3.37% and 2.92% of the displayed totals. (source blocks: H0123, C0007, H0206)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly limit routing to predefined expert roles and legal actions, so the evaluated framework does not establish adaptation through creation of new experts or dynamic role reconfiguration. (source blocks: H0173)
- The authors acknowledge that review and revision demand can erode efficiency; natural-trace error prevalence and observed escalation fractions are not numerically reported, limiting extrapolation of the cascade's savings to other workload distributions. (source blocks: H0173, H0199, H0202)
- The inspected paper reports fixed-subset point estimates without confidence intervals, paired significance tests or repeated-run variability, so small accuracy differences and latency advantages should not be interpreted as established non-inferiority or stable population effects. (source blocks: H0204, T0009, T0010, T0016, T0017, H0123)
- Complete prompts and implementation details are deferred to an uninspected repository; the paper alone does not fully specify decoding settings, exact API versions, checkpoint rules, corruption generation, screening denominators, the ASR definition or the model-price schedule, and no replication is claimed here. (source blocks: H0105, H0115, H0171, H0210, H0206, T0016)
- I3 accuracy evaluates integrable/non-integrable classification rather than the scientific merit of generated research proposals, and the controlled error-injection study does not establish reliability against naturally distributed errors or adaptive adversaries. (source blocks: H0205, H0115, H0202, C0002)

## Critical interpretation

- Preserving artifact content is a well-motivated architectural contribution, and the router-only masking ablation supports its relevance; however, changed token use and execution trajectories mean that nearly equal call counts do not isolate a pure per-decision causal effect. (source blocks: H0062, H0069, T0015, C0004)
- The API-Bank feedback-ablation discrepancy should be corrected or explained by the authors: the introduction states 9.02 points, whereas Table 2(b)'s displayed 66.29% and 57.30% imply 8.99 points; this review preserves the contradiction rather than inventing a reconciliation. (source blocks: T0015, H0013)
- The principal efficiency comparison is specifically against LAS, not an across-the-board optimum: MetaGPT achieves higher DeepSeek I3 accuracy at higher token and call cost, and GPT-5 MATH shows that lower generative-token use need not produce lower end-to-end latency. (source blocks: T0010, H0109)
- Table 4 appropriately separates screening rates from workflow outcomes, but missing screening denominators and corruption details prevent a precise uncertainty or calibration assessment; low false-positive rates alone are especially insufficient when the SLM judge also has substantially lower injected-error detection. (source blocks: T0017, H0115)
- The cascade equations are useful conditional accounting identities, not empirical guarantees of whole-workflow savings or safety: missed errors bypass the generative reviewer, and revisions, repeated checks and natural artifact quality can change both resource use and downstream failure. (source blocks: H0079, H0173, H0199, H0202)
- Excluding decision-model tokens makes the main token reductions measures of generative workload rather than total computation; the separate request-time breakdown helps, but deployment-wide cost claims still require explicit pricing and execution conditions rather than token counts alone. (source blocks: H0206, H0105, H0123, C0007)
