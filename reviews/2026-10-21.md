# HydraPoT: A Configurable Multi-Agent Framework for Cost-Aware LLM-Assisted Honeypots

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27722v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- HydraPoT proposes configurable per-command routing among a static honeypot, a lightweight LLM, and a cloud LLM, with shared session state intended to reduce inconsistencies when the responding agent changes. (source blocks: H0009, H0033, H0056, H0058)
- Its principal contribution is the integration of heterogeneous cost-aware routing and shared-state prompting; the paper's own related work already includes deterministic/LLM hybrids and multi-agent response verification. (source blocks: H0009, H0020, H0033)
- The evaluated policy demonstrates a workload-specific marginal-cost versus response-fidelity trade-off, rather than establishing an optimal routing strategy, universal cross-agent consistency, or operational deception effectiveness. (source blocks: H0069, H0085, T0003, H0095, T0005, T0006, H0108)

## Methods

- The implementation routes commands through five orchestration components and models working directory, files, packages, users/groups, security settings, and service status in a per-session SYSTEM\_STATE dictionary used to construct LLM prompts. (source blocks: H0033, H0055, H0056, H0058)
- Command state impact is used as a proxy for emulation complexity; in the case study, state-independent F0 commands go to Cowrie, state-dependent F0–F2 commands to the local model, and F3–F4 commands to the cloud model. (source blocks: H0050, T0001, H0085)
- The reported LLM setup uses Qwen3.5-9B GGUF Q5\_K\_M locally with an 8192-token context window, 256-token generation limit, temperature 0, and repetition penalty 1.15, alongside the DeepSeek-V4 Flash cloud API. (source blocks: H0062)
- Architectural overhead is measured on 480 Linux command records using fixed routing to each agent, while context preservation uses 29 creator–reader command pairs, Ubuntu 22.04 reference outputs, and three runs per reported comparison. (source blocks: H0065, H0066, H0068, H0072)
- The fidelity case study replays 2,179 commands from 109 attacker sessions against three single-agent comparators, using six reference-output similarity metrics and a GPT-5 realism judge that receives the host profile and SYSTEM\_STATE but not the reference output. (source blocks: H0085, H0086, T0003, T0004, H0093)
- Cost accounting sums cloud token charges and measured local-inference electricity estimates, treating Cowrie computation as negligible; it is not a full deployment-cost model. (source blocks: H0095, T0006)

## Author-reported results

- For the 480-record fixed-routing overhead experiment, Figure 2 labels standalone/HydraPoT runtimes as 68/71 seconds for Cowrie, 1,529/1,562 seconds for the local LLM, and 1,385/1,417 seconds for the cloud LLM; these rounded values imply approximately 4.4%, 2.2%, and 2.3% overhead, respectively, and the caption reports SD below 1% without a repeat count. (source blocks: H0065, H0066, C0003)
- Across the 29-pair context benchmark averaged over three runs, Table 2 reports disabled-to-enabled preservation rates of 17.2% to 100% for Cowrie→cloud, 79.3% to 100% for Cowrie→local, and 14.9% to 100% for both cloud→local and local→cloud; the largest reported gain is 85.1 percentage points, and Cowrie-reader transitions are excluded. (source blocks: H0068, H0069, T0002, H0072)
- In the 2,179-command case study, Table 3 reports HydraPoT versus cloud-only scores of 0.662 versus 0.699 for cosine similarity, 0.642 versus 0.691 for BERTScore, 0.643 versus 0.682 for Levenshtein, 0.766 versus 0.795 for Jaro–Winkler, 0.450 versus 0.487 for BLEU, and 0.286 versus 0.311 for Exact Match; HydraPoT exceeds both lower-cost baselines in every aggregate metric, with no reported uncertainty intervals. (source blocks: H0085, H0086, T0003)
- Figure 4 reports average judge scores of 3.20 for Cowrie, 3.40 for the local model, 4.19 for cloud-only, and 3.94 for HydraPoT on the 1–5 rubric, giving the reported 0.25-point cloud/HydraPoT difference; these are automated realism judgments rather than human deception measurements. (source blocks: T0004, H0093, H0094, C0014, H0108)
- For the same case-study workload, Table 5 reports cloud-routed commands falling from 2,179 to 531 and cloud-processed tokens from 11,802,012 to 3,238,807, a saving of 8,563,205 tokens or 72.6% after rounding. (source blocks: H0085, T0005)
- Table 6 reports $1.98 total marginal cost for cloud-only and $0.5415 for HydraPoT, comprising $0.54 cloud charges and $0.0015 electricity; their ratio is approximately 3.66, consistent with the authors' rounded 3.7-fold cost comparison. (source blocks: H0095, T0006, H0100)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly leave human evaluation, real-world deployment, and prompt-injection mitigation to future work, so the paper does not establish effectiveness or safety against adaptive live attackers. (source blocks: H0108)
- The evaluated Cowrie version cannot read externally maintained session state, restricting the context-preservation evidence to the four reported directed transitions and preventing a general claim of consistency across all heterogeneous-agent transitions. (source blocks: H0069, T0002)
- Success on two-command stateful pairs does not establish long-session consistency, and the paper does not report pair-level matching rules, uncertainty intervals, or robustness to accumulated state-extraction errors. (source blocks: H0056, H0068, H0072)
- Fidelity and cost evidence comes from one routing policy and one local/cloud model pairing; per-impact sample counts, session-clustered uncertainty, judge-repeat variability, and alternative-policy comparisons are not reported. (source blocks: H0062, H0085, T0003, H0093, C0014, C0011)
- Cost estimates omit full provisioning and operating expenses, and the inspected paper does not provide the energy-measurement details, numerical electricity tariff, or input/output token-price breakdown needed to reconstruct its dollar estimates independently. (source blocks: H0095, T0006, H0100)
- The linked implementation and pricing artifacts were not inspected here, leaving artifact completeness and exact cost reproducibility unverified; no experiments or independent replication were performed in this review. (source blocks: H0095, H0010)

## Critical interpretation

- The architecture offers a plausible engineering trade-off, but state impact should be validated as a predictor of emulation difficulty rather than assumed to be one; argument-sensitive taxonomy entries also require explicit classification and state-dependence rules. (source blocks: H0009, H0050, T0001, H0085)
- A complete account of state extraction, failed-command handling, and validation is important because SYSTEM\_STATE is updated from generated command-response pairs; an incorrect response could otherwise become authoritative context for subsequent agents. (source blocks: H0055, H0056, H0058)
- The overhead experiment isolates fixed-route framework processing but does not measure actual switching overhead, concurrent-session behavior, or tail latency, and the figure's per-session axis wording should be clarified against the text's 480-record workload description. (source blocks: H0065, H0066, C0003)
- The authors' 'within 8%' summary is best treated as approximate: the rounded aggregate scores imply relative deficits of about 3.65–8.04%, while the inspected per-level plots show a larger cloud advantage at F1; neither aggregate proximity nor matching high-impact bars establishes statistical equivalence. (source blocks: T0003, H0089, C0014, C0011)
- The judge's perfect scores on genuine outputs provide only positive-example calibration, and access to SYSTEM\_STATE could reward internal plausibility rather than independently correct state; human agreement, corrupted-state controls, and verified-state judging would strengthen the realism conclusions. (source blocks: H0056, T0004, H0093, H0108)
- The demonstrated savings are convincing as reported marginal accounting for this replay, but a policy sweep with equal-budget comparators and complete cost assumptions is needed before claiming a generally favorable deployment frontier. (source blocks: H0085, T0003, H0095, T0005, T0006)
