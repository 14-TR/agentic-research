# Agentic Orchestration of EDC Data Space Retrieval and Natural Language Driven Data Processing

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26548v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The paper presents a natural-language interface to EDC data-space negotiations, using LangGraph to coordinate extraction, machine-learning and visualization workers with orchestration and verification agents. (source blocks: H0029, C0002, H0036)
- Its central engineering contribution is the integration of agent-based offer selection across geography, budget, usage and entity constraints with credential-based provider enforcement, rather than a demonstrated formal guarantee from LLM reasoning. (source blocks: H0040, T0001, H0027, H0077)
- The evidence supports a bounded prototype demonstration of negotiation and task completion; it does not establish general data sovereignty, post-transfer usage control, or usability for real non-expert users. (source blocks: H0051, H0076, H0075, H0074)

## Methods

- The authors deploy a local, containerized data space with three EDC providers and one consumer, using Kubernetes in Docker, LangGraph and the locally hosted Magistral-Small-2509 model. (source blocks: H0026, H0036, C0002)
- Provider policies are represented across four dimensions, with agent-selected offers passed to credential-checked DSP negotiation; the original policy table was inspected rather than interpreted solely from its flattened extraction. (source blocks: H0040, T0001, H0027)
- The primary benchmark crosses five scenarios, four worker-activation levels and three prompt styles, repeating each of the 60 configurations ten times for 600 end-to-end runs. (source blocks: H0046, H0051)
- Success is trace-based and scenario-specific: admissible requests require correct routing, verifier clearance and downstream completion, whereas unsatisfiable requests require clean refusal without leakage; scripted answers simulate user clarification. (source blocks: H0049, H0075, H0060)
- The separate 600-run geographic track pre-satisfies non-geographic credentials and cycles through five consumer locations; its unequal location-by-scenario cell sizes are documented, yielding 270 eligible, 210 geographically blocked and 120 no-provider runs. (source blocks: H0048, H0068, C0009)

## Author-reported results

- The authors report 88.7% overall task success across the 600 primary runs, with a reported 95% confidence interval of 85.9–91.0%; 23 of 60 configurations had mixed repetition outcomes, and average per-configuration Wilson uncertainty was ±17.5 percentage points. (source blocks: H0051, C0007)
- Author-reported whole-percent success summaries are 98% for technical, 96% for expert and 72% for casual prompts, each pooling 200 primary runs; these results apply to scripted benchmark interactions, not measured human-user performance. (source blocks: H0053, H0046, H0051, C0004, H0075)
- The primary failure chart reports 24 clarification give-ups, 21 over-refusals, two wrong-provider cases, 14 partial deliveries, two unverified/no-artifact cases and five incomplete reject cases, with no recorded guardrail leaks or timeout/execution failures; separately, one policy-valid TC4 choice failed cheapest-provider optimization. (source blocks: C0007, C0006, H0057, H0061, H0060)
- Reported median run latency is 57 seconds for extraction alone, 90 seconds with ML, 76 seconds with visualization and 123 seconds for the full pipeline; the supplied boxplots also show substantial right-tail variability. (source blocks: H0063, C0008)
- In the geographic track, the supplied figures show delivered-and-verified success for 231 of 270 eligible runs, reported as 86%; this denominator excludes geographically blocked and no-provider requests. (source blocks: H0068, C0009, C0010)
- The authors report that EDC prevented actual transfers in all 210 geographically disallowed and 120 no-provider runs, despite false successful-verification reports in two and one runs respectively; the reported absence of transfer is not an independent log audit by this review. (source blocks: H0069, C0009, C0010, H0049)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors tested one small local model and three providers hosting familiar open datasets; robustness across model families, quantization settings and operationally heterogeneous catalogs remains unestablished. (source blocks: H0073, H0074)
- Clarification responses were scripted and there was no user study, so casual-prompt failures cannot be directly generalized to real non-expert interactions. (source blocks: H0075, H0060, H0054)
- Tested policies use single-valued constraints in four dimensions and exclude logical compositions, temporal conditions and conflicting permissions; post-negotiation data usage is explicitly outside scope. (source blocks: H0076, H0040)
- The LLM verifier offers no formal compliance guarantee, shares a model with the agents it audits and checks downstream completion rather than ML or visualization quality. (source blocks: H0077)
- The threat model assumes honest users and providers, while catalog text is a recognized prompt-injection channel; local inference alone does not establish adversarial security or comprehensive privacy. (source blocks: H0078, H0036, H0027)
- Source wording remains inconsistent: the geographic results say no prohibited contracts were made, while the limitations refer to approved contracts violating geography; both attribute the anomaly to verification hallucinations and report no resulting transfer, so actual prohibited contract formation is not established. (source blocks: H0069, H0077, C0010)

## Critical interpretation

- The integration is technically coherent, but the evaluation does not isolate the benefit of multi-agent orchestration over a single-agent or deterministic policy-selection baseline; the results demonstrate prototype operation rather than comparative superiority. (source blocks: H0029, C0002, H0046, H0051)
- The prompt-style comparison confounds linguistic register with supplied information and scripted clarification quality: the technical example includes explicit dataset, budget and training specifications absent from its casual counterpart. (source blocks: H0054, H0053, H0075)
- The strongest observed safety boundary is the provider's credential-based enforcement, not the LLM verifier; zero observed leaks under the tested conditions should not be promoted to a universal policy-compliance guarantee. (source blocks: H0027, H0060, H0069, H0077)
- Routing validity, economic optimality and end-to-end completion need separate metrics: Figure 4 colors the expensive TC4 selection as correct, while the prose calls it an optimization failure, and Figure 5 additionally captures failures after correct extraction. (source blocks: C0006, H0057, C0007, H0061, H0049)
- The geographic matrix must be read as expected eligibility plus reported success counts, not as direct proof of actual access: three red cells contain apparent successes that the summary legend and prose identify as false verification without transfer. (source blocks: C0009, C0010, H0069)
- Latency medians and the pooled success interval are useful descriptive summaries, but they do not establish marginal agent cost, production scalability or deployment-wide reliability; long latency tails, broad per-configuration uncertainty and uninspected underlying telemetry limit those inferences. (source blocks: H0063, C0008, H0051, H0049)
