# Adaptive Skill Granularity for Coding Agents: Runtime Routing and Macro-to-Micro Recovery

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31461v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The paper proposes controlling coding-agent execution granularity separately from skill retrieval, by restricting actions to primitive tools, macro skills, or macro execution with fallback. (source blocks: H0012, H0040)
- The implemented adaptive behavior combines pre-execution routing with an event-triggered, one-way macro-to-micro downgrade, rather than unrestricted switching between abstraction levels. (source blocks: H0040, H0061, H0069, H0075)
- The authors' pilot results support a task- and failure-timing-dependent trade-off between fixed micro execution, fixed macro execution, and recovery-enabled execution, not universal superiority of adaptive routing. (source blocks: T0014, C0004, T0015, C0005, T0017, C0007)
- Learned routing and selective fallback are promising within the reported pilot, but optimal routing, robustness across repositories and models, and automated macro evolution are not established by these experiments. (source blocks: H0076, H0088, T0016, C0006, H0138, H0123)

## Methods

- Episodes operate on isolated task workspaces with preserved initial snapshots; the agent emits one JSON action per step, and task-specific validators determine success from the final workspace. (source blocks: H0096, H0097)
- Initial routing uses pre-execution prompt, metadata, and workspace features describing files, AST structure, imports, dependencies, and task-type flags; the design excludes current-run verifier outcomes from this initial decision. (source blocks: H0061, H0073)
- The learned mode router is an offline-trained decision tree with maximum depth 4 and deterministic JSON-tree inference; the paper also describes a separate recovery router but incompletely specifies its training and runtime decision procedure. (source blocks: H0076, H0075)
- The prototype uses three hand-written macros, with contracts for applicability, side effects, verification, failure behavior, and fallback; enabled recovery continues with primitive tools from the current, potentially modified workspace. (source blocks: H0069, H0081, H0086)
- Comparisons separate fixed granularity, initial routing without recovery, heuristic routing with FSM downgrade, and the full learned controller; the authors report shared underlying tools and final-workspace verifiers. (source blocks: H0097, H0105, T0014, T0016, T0017)
- The evaluation defines cost per success using tokens from all runs, including failures, and uses G-score only as a secondary comparison for similarly successful policies; reported tables additionally present mean costs and process rates. (source blocks: H0107, H0054, T0014, T0015, T0016, T0017)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly defer broader task, repository, failure-mode, and model coverage; the reported experiments use 12-task and seven-task pilots, whereas the benchmark table lists target counts of 20 per family rather than completed evaluation counts. (source blocks: T0011, C0004, C0005, C0006, C0007, H0138)
- The authors explicitly leave end-to-end macro evolution to future work, and the current evidence concerns three deliberately small, hand-written macros rather than automatically acquired skill libraries. (source blocks: H0081, H0088, C0001)
- Reviewer-identified reporting gaps include the underlying LLM identity and version, decoding settings, run repetitions, training-set size, held-out task or repository splits, and sufficient recovery-router details; these are not established by the inspected paper, and the linked code remains unverified here. (source blocks: H0076, H0075, H0096, H0097, H0002)
- Although the protocol description promises confidence intervals and transition correctness, the inspected results provide neither intervals nor numerical transition-correctness estimates, and the aggregation populations for table-labelled mean costs and steps are insufficiently specified. (source blocks: H0107, T0014, T0015, T0016, T0017, H0016)
- The source contains unresolved presentation inconsistencies: After no-recovery Mean Tokens is 2440.14 in Table 3 but 2280.00 in Table 5, and Figure 3 labels comparators as oracle routes where the tables identify heuristic variants; neither difference can be reconciled from the supplied evidence. (source blocks: T0015, T0017, C0008)
- The deterministic final-workspace validator establishes success only under its task-specific checks; terminal macro-failure status, actual recovery success, and verifier acceptance are distinct outcomes that the paper does not consistently separate. (source blocks: H0069, H0086, H0097, C0002, H0119)

## Critical interpretation

- The explicit action restrictions, preserved workspaces, and logged downgrade transitions provide a useful auditable mechanism, but comparisons against forced micro-only and macro-only agents do not establish advantage over an unconstrained agent that can already choose primitive tools and macros; the paper itself reserves that baseline and an LLM router for the full study. (source blocks: H0012, H0069, H0096, H0097, H0016)
- A shallow decision tree does not by itself prevent memorization, and perfect agreement with empirical labels cannot establish optimal mode selection without task-disjoint evaluation and a documented label-construction procedure. (source blocks: H0076, T0016, C0006, H0123)
- The full controller changes both initial mode selection and recovery enablement, so its reduced costs cannot be attributed solely to a better failure-time recovery decision; Figure 3's micro-only successes make this confounding directly visible. (source blocks: H0075, H0105, T0017, C0008)
- Controlled Before and After injections isolate an informative timing effect, but they do not establish a general robustness guarantee for partial edits, schema failures, or executor exceptions; reporting outcomes by the proposed failure taxonomy would make the recovery claim more diagnostic. (source blocks: H0069, H0086, T0015, C0005, H0119)
- Success and failure-inclusive cost per success should dominate efficiency comparisons: Table 2's higher macro G-score accompanies substantially lower success, while missing Before no-recovery costs and unclear mean-cost denominators prevent a complete accounting of failed-run expenditure. (source blocks: H0107, H0054, T0014, T0015, T0017)
- Claims of significant savings, optimal routing, and guaranteed robustness should be narrowed to descriptive pilot findings until uncertainty, generalization, and comparator accounting are established; the conflicting baseline costs and oracle-versus-heuristic labels must remain explicit rather than silently corrected. (source blocks: T0015, T0017, C0007, H0016, H0138, C0008, H0123)
