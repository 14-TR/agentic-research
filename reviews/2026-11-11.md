# DrugCoding: A Scientific Programming Agent for Drug Discovery

https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13651v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The authors propose DrugCoding as a scientific-agent architecture that separates context resolution, dependency-aware planning, scientific-model recommendation, execution, and evidence synthesis. (source blocks: H0022, H0029, H0031, C0002)
- The described routing mechanism separates evidence-based scientific preference from execution eligibility and final model binding; recommendation scores are not calibrated success probabilities. (source blocks: H0045, H0047)
- The empirical evidence supports reliable reference-matching computational submissions on the evaluated multi-turn benchmark, not independently established scientific execution or drug-discovery efficacy. (source blocks: H0068, T0003, T0004, H0090)

## Methods

- The proposed workflow links extracted scientific entities to source records and constructs plans containing input references, predecessor relationships, and downstream-expansion conditions. (source blocks: H0029, H0031)
- Scientific recommendations use supplied evidence without live retrieval, while the execution layer checks input contracts and runtime eligibility before selecting a binding; output verification is deterministic contract checking. (source blocks: H0045, H0047, H0053)
- M-TCDD comprises 1,250 complete multi-turn sessions containing 2,500 action-level inputs across eight tool interfaces. (source blocks: H0063)
- The approximately ten-percent subset contains 128 sessions and 256 actions, selected within final-tool groups by a weighted feature-percentile score with upward-rounded quotas and a minimum of five sessions per group. (source blocks: H0063, H0064)
- Full-benchmark comparisons use seven backbone configurations and adapted coding-agent pipelines; subset comparisons use three configuration labels with Claude Science accessed through the web and Rosalind Workbench through a Codex CLI skill configuration. (source blocks: H0066, T0003, T0004)
- Overall Pass@1 is binary session-level exact match over all required operation identities, ordering, and normalized arguments, whereas Figure 1 subtasks score individual actions and Figure 3 separates priority-classified evaluation failures from overlapping action-field errors. (source blocks: H0068, C0007, C0001)

## Author-reported results

- On M-TCDD, the authors report mean session Pass@1 of 97.35% for DrugCoding, 88.11% for Codex, 87.05% for Claude Code, and 85.26% for Kimi across seven configurations, each evaluated on 1,250 sessions; the rounded mean advantage over Codex is 9.24 percentage points, with no uncertainty intervals reported. (source blocks: H0063, T0003)
- On the 128-session subset, the authors report mean session Pass@1 of 97.92%, 96.61%, and 96.09% for DrugCoding, Claude Science, and Rosalind Workbench across three configuration labels; DrugCoding ties Claude Science at 96.09% in the GPT-5.6-Sol row, and no uncertainty intervals are reported. (source blocks: H0064, H0066, T0004)
- The M-TCDD ablation reports mean Pass@1 of 96.79% for single-agent and 97.35% for multi-agent DrugCoding, a 0.56-percentage-point difference in rounded means; multi-agent improves four configurations but regresses in three, with GPT-5.6-Sol increasing from 92.16% to 98.40%. (source blocks: T0005, H0079)
- Figure 1 reports DrugCoding leading all eight action-level task panels averaged across seven configurations, including drug–drug interaction prediction at 96.90% versus 79.29% for the strongest displayed baseline, Claude Code, and affinity classification at 96.43% versus 81.32% for Claude Code; these are submission exact-match scores rather than predictive-model accuracies. (source blocks: H0068, C0001)
- The failure analysis reports 232, 1,040, 1,133, and 1,290 failed session evaluations for DrugCoding, Codex, Claude Code, and Kimi across the seven full-benchmark configurations, and eight, 13, and 15 for DrugCoding, Claude Science, and Rosalind Workbench across the three subset configurations. (source blocks: H0063, H0064, T0003, T0004, H0083, H0084, C0007)
- The illustrated cases show DrugCoding preserving a 36-entry editing mask while Codex and Claude Code submit 37 and 38 entries, and preserving the displayed molecular-string suffix while Claude Science and Rosalind Workbench append two characters; the latter case is annotated as a change from 44 to 46 characters. (source blocks: H0088)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors explicitly leave the transfer from correctly structured submissions to executed workflows and validated scientific outcomes for further evaluation; the reported Pass@1 scores should not be presented as biological validation. (source blocks: H0068, H0090)
- The authors acknowledge that prompting, provider routes, and output handling vary across configurations, preventing causal attribution of input-preservation differences to a particular architectural component. (source blocks: H0084)
- Reviewer assessment: the performance tables supply point estimates without confidence intervals, repeated-run variability, or paired significance analyses, so the precision and robustness of small average gains remain unestablished. (source blocks: T0003, T0004, T0005)
- Reviewer assessment: the subset-selection description omits numerical feature weights and does not establish that its proxy ranking identifies the empirically hardest sessions; its findings apply to the specified within-tool selected subset. (source blocks: H0063, H0064)
- Reviewer assessment: the subset configuration labels do not by themselves establish equivalent control over the underlying models in the web-based and CLI-based scientific-agent pipelines. (source blocks: H0066, T0004)
- The source explicitly states that report synthesis lacks an independent language-model review, and its deterministic artifact checks should not be interpreted as an independent assessment of scientific truth. (source blocks: H0053, H0037)

## Critical interpretation

- The strongest demonstrated contribution is preservation of scientific arguments under strict multi-turn exact-match scoring; the architectural execution schematic is substantially broader than the measured endpoint. (source blocks: H0068, H0083, H0084, H0090, C0002)
- The introduction says scientific-model selection considers computational cost, but the detailed routing method explicitly excludes resource cost from scientific ranking; this unresolved source inconsistency precludes a claim of demonstrated cost-aware routing. (source blocks: H0013, H0045, H0047)
- The mixed ablation results argue against a universal multi-agent advantage, and the reported comparison does not isolate routing, provenance handling, verification, and orchestration as separate causes of improvement. (source blocks: T0005, H0079, H0084)
- Baseline adaptation and heterogeneous access routes leave protocol conversion, parsing, and provider behavior as plausible alternative explanations for part of the advantage; matched interfaces and component-level controls would distinguish these from architectural effects. (source blocks: H0066, H0084, C0007)
- Reproducibility would be strengthened by explicit subset feature weights, controlled model/version and interface records, paired session outcomes, and repeated-run uncertainty rather than relying solely on rounded aggregate means. (source blocks: H0063, H0064, H0066, T0003, T0004, T0005)
- The figures are consistent with the reported aggregate comparisons, but the performance chart's truncated vertical axes visually amplify differences and the selected mask/string examples cannot establish general scientific validity or population-wide error mechanisms. (source blocks: C0001, H0088, H0090)
