# PAUSE: Search-Free Steering of Coding Agents Before Their First Source-Code Edit

https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29870v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- PAUSE's central contribution is an action-defined opportunity for one planning intervention: it detects a qualifying editor request without model-based timing inference, then gives the existing Executor external guidance while retaining its state; search-free does not mean inference-free. (source blocks: H0045, H0047, H0057, H0061)
- The motivating trajectory analysis reports a first qualifying source-edit request at a median 60.0% of normalized action progress among 476 of 500 recorded Qwen3-coder runs with such a request; this observation describes an investigation opportunity, not a universal optimal stopping point. (source blocks: H0014, C0002, H0154, H0158)
- The inspected evidence supports useful online steering and eligible-failure recovery in the tested settings, but the benefits depend on Planner quality and harness behavior, and do not establish statistically significant superiority over LivePlan, equivalence to SICA, or optimal intervention timing. (source blocks: T0006, H0102, H0103, T0007, T0009, T0010, H0158)

## Methods

- Online PAUSE blocks the first editor request targeting a predesignated repository source path, excludes the pending response and edit payload from planning evidence, and asks a separate tool-free Planner for a natural-language repair plan from the task and rendered completed transcript, omitting editor actions. (source blocks: H0045, H0050, H0057, H0059)
- The plan is appended as a user message to the same Executor, which preserves its completed conversation and workspace, chooses a new action rather than automatically executing the blocked edit, and continues without further PAUSE interception. (source blocks: H0061, C0003)
- Offline recovery restores the last explicit think checkpoint before the first qualifying edit, retains that think for the Executor but excludes it from the Planner's input, replays the prefix in a fresh container, rejects fidelity failures, and resumes with the original remaining iteration budget. (source blocks: H0067, H0069)
- The study evaluates 500 Verified tasks and 300 Multilingual tasks, primarily using one designated attempt per task and benchmark evaluation with exact McNemar comparisons; SWE-agent comparisons allow 100 Executor calls and 14,400 seconds, and reported dollar costs cover only Planner inference. (source blocks: H0076, H0080, H0081, T0005, H0092)
- Controls compare no-plan continuation, self-reconsideration, different Planners, and resume versus restart; resume and restart have different available execution budgets, whereas checkpoint arms receive matched fresh iterations but independently generated plans, and the successful-donor diagnostic uses three attempts per donor–recipient pair. (source blocks: H0093, T0007, T0008, H0136, H0137)

## Author-reported results

- With Qwen3-coder and GPT-5.6 Sol in SWE-agent, the authors report PAUSE resolving 325/500 Verified tasks versus baseline 251/500, and 129/300 Multilingual tasks versus 85/300: gains of 14.8 and 14.7 percentage points. PAUSE exceeds LivePlan's 309/500 and 118/300, but the reported paired differences are nonsignificant, p=0.11 and p=0.18. (source blocks: T0006, H0102)
- SICA has higher observed resolution than PAUSE, 338/500 versus 325/500 on Verified and 133/300 versus 129/300 on Multilingual, with reported p=0.18 and p=0.71; PAUSE's Planner bills are $26.13 versus $231.71 and $23.68 versus $159.63, approximately 11% and 15% of SICA's, while its Executor token totals are also lower in these SWE-agent comparisons. (source blocks: H0080, T0006, H0103)
- Among eligible recorded failures in OpenHands, GPT-5.6 Sol guidance recovers 79/223 Qwen3-coder failures (35.4%) and 53/174 Devstral-small failures (30.5%); no-plan continuation recovers 9/223 and 14/174, self-reconsideration 13/223 and 17/174, and an Executor-model Planner 12/223 and 20/174. (source blocks: T0007)
- The separate full-benchmark offline table reports GPT-5.6 Sol resume at 291/500 resolved (58.2%) versus baseline 228/500 (45.6%) and restart 293/500 (58.6%); its additional Executor tokens are 347.4M versus restart's 519.1M, about 33% fewer, while Qwen3-Thinking resume uses 375.7M versus 641.6M, about 41% fewer, but remains below baseline resolution. Including the original run, GPT resume totals 1,139.3M Executor tokens versus SHERLOC's 703.9M. (source blocks: T0008, H0119, H0114)
- With the Qwen3-coder/GPT-5.6 Sol pair fixed on Verified, online gains are 14.8 percentage points in SWE-agent, 6.0 in Pi, and 16.8 in OpenHands; Executor tokens decrease only in SWE-agent and increase in Pi and OpenHands. OpenHands think-stripped planning solves 318/500 versus 311/500 with the full transcript, a nonsignificant difference reported at p=0.48. (source blocks: T0009, H0132)
- On 161 failures graded across all four checkpoint arms, the default offline checkpoint recovers 56 versus 43 at the 20% cut and 50 at each immediate-pre-edit and 90% cut; the larger pairwise comparisons report p=0.12, 0.39, and 0.62. A later-edit check recovers 15 tasks in each arm among 29 paired runs, while successful-donor guidance yields at least one success in three attempts for 64/97 donor–recipient pairs, a distinct diagnostic population. (source blocks: T0010, H0136, H0137, H0142)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors acknowledge that a tool-free Planner cannot verify missing repository evidence and that its single plan may become obsolete as the Executor obtains new observations; repair success still depends on implementation and validation by the Executor. (source blocks: H0146, H0147)
- Coverage depends on interceptable editor requests and repository-specific path rules, so shell-based modifications can bypass or precede the trigger; the frequency of this behavior in Pi was not measured, leaving its proposed explanation for the smaller Pi gain unquantified. (source blocks: H0045, H0148, H0156, H0129)
- Offline recovery applies only to trajectories with usable reasoning checkpoints and faithful restoration, and its recovery rates should not be generalized to excluded failures; cross-language evidence is confined to online SWE-agent, while checkpoint ablations use Qwen3-coder on Verified rather than broader development workflows. (source blocks: H0067, H0069, H0149, H0154)
- Public benchmark training exposure remains unknown, most comparisons use one attempt per task, and benchmark-test resolution does not establish correctness beyond test coverage; nonsignificant differences and similar counts do not establish equivalence. (source blocks: H0092, H0152, H0156, H0158)
- Implementation, prompts, trajectories, and per-task accounting were not inspected here, and the source alternates between describing released scripts and promising future release; reported aggregates and statistical tests therefore remain author results rather than independently reproduced findings. (source blocks: H0169, H0071, H0081)

## Critical interpretation

- The strongest design feature is the restored-state comparison of external planning with no-plan continuation and self-reconsideration: it addresses whether another execution opportunity alone explains recovery, while the much weaker Executor-model Planner result shows that assigning a planning role is not sufficient by itself. (source blocks: T0007, H0067, H0069)
- The broad effectiveness language should be narrowed to higher observed resolution than LivePlan and a favorable resource–resolution trade-off against SICA, not demonstrated superiority or equivalence; moreover, calling Multilingual Planner costs similar obscures a $23.68 versus $16.41 bill, and advisor output limits differ across methods. (source blocks: T0006, H0102, H0103, T0005)
- The boundary experiments support practicality rather than optimality: every checkpoint receives a different plan, all primary paired timing differences are nonsignificant, restoration selects the analyzed cohorts, and successful same-task donor guidance is a privileged, multi-attempt diagnostic rather than a deployable planning comparison. (source blocks: T0010, H0136, H0137, H0142, H0158)
- Offline outcome accounting needs a cohort-explicit reconciliation: Table 5's separate evaluation reports 291 successes, whereas the 79 recovered failures and 10 regressions on the common 178-success subgroup cannot simply be combined with 228 baseline successes to derive that number. This is not proof that Table 5 is wrong, but the subgroup regression analysis cannot substantiate complete accounting or a general claim that guidance never adds regressions. (source blocks: T0007, T0008, H0120, H0114, H0152)
- The resource result is best framed as cheaper additional recovery from an existing trajectory: resume changes both state retention and available budget relative to restart, reported dollars exclude Executor inference, and counting the original investigation reverses the apparent Executor-token advantage over SHERLOC. (source blocks: H0080, H0093, T0008, H0119)
- The inspected subgroup figures show meaningful heterogeneity rather than uniform benefit: PAUSE leads the 45-task hard Verified group but loses ground to baseline on Rust, and the language connecting bars are method ranges rather than uncertainty intervals; these descriptive slices merit repeated, adequately powered analysis before supporting mechanism or subgroup superiority claims. (source blocks: C0007, C0008, H0105, H0092)
