# The Two-Ledger Model: Transaction-Grain Cost Accounting for Multi-Agent AI Systems Across Clouds, Models, and Systems of Record

https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06989v1

Scientific review with model interpretation. Author-reported results are not independent replication.
No experiments or reproductions were executed by this review workflow.

Coverage: methods, results and limitations checked; tables: checked; figures: checked.

## Author claims

- The paper's central argument is that settlement aggregation cannot recover enterprise transaction assignments when the transaction identifiers are absent from provider-visible billing dimensions; requester identity alone does not supply the missing transaction boundary. (source blocks: H0058, H0059)
- The proposed Two-Ledger Model separates invoice-facing settlement records from invocation-grain runtime attribution, joining them through correlation, allocation, a versioned rate card, and period reconciliation rather than treating a gateway as the entire accounting system. (source blocks: H0080, H0074, H0118, C0003, C0005)
- A useful design feature is explicit assignment provenance: trace context, protocol metadata, business keys, and probabilistic matching form a fallback hierarchy, while uncertain records can be pooled instead of forcibly assigned to a transaction. (source blocks: T0006, H0097)
- The paper proposes business-process cost roll-ups and versioned agentic cost metrics, together with a FOCUS-shaped invocation profile; these are architecture and standardization proposals rather than demonstrated adoption or ratified new standards. (source blocks: H0080, H0076, H0110, H0132, H0172)

## Methods

- Runtime records are proposed primarily at an enterprise LLM gateway, supplemented by SDK/sidecar emission and provider-API backfill; the intended ledger excludes prompts and responses but retains usage, identities, timings, status, and business correlation metadata. (source blocks: H0074, H0076, T0006)
- The cost model sums token usage multiplied by effective rates, tool fees, and allocated shared-resource costs, with commitment consumption normalized in the provider's own meter; classification-dependent token efficiency, coordination overhead, and rework accompany transaction-cost quantiles. (source blocks: H0101, H0103, H0110)
- Period reconciliation compares billed and attributed amounts within provider–SKU–period groups, then attempts to separate rate drift, timing, idle capacity, and unmetered usage; the two-sided extension also checks uncleared accruals and inconsistent billed prices. (source blocks: H0119, H0118, H0143)
- The synthetic study generates 20,000 transactions and 109,792 model invocations, varies trace and business-event availability, retains protocol metadata on half the otherwise untraced calls, and calibrates probabilistic correlation on a separate 4,000-transaction sample. (source blocks: H0136, T0014)
- Evaluation combines a qualitative requirements comparison, a synthetic accounting walkthrough, correlation sweeps against trace-only attribution, planted billing discrepancies, and a gating forecaster trained on 12,000 separate trajectories and tested on 20,000 transactions. (source blocks: H0143, H0149, H0132, H0131, H0121)

## Author-reported results

- In the authors' main E1 sweep, with business-event availability 0.85 and confidence floor 0.80, spine cost coverage stays above 99% as trace availability falls to zero; at zero trace availability, Table VIII reports 99.8% coverage, 95.6% cost accuracy, and 4.44% of total spend misattributed, versus 0.0% trace-only coverage. (source blocks: H0136, T0014, C0011)
- At trace availability 0.4 and floor 0.80, removing business-event feeds changes the reported misattributed-spend share from 2.62% at event availability 0.85 to 14.21% at zero event availability; the no-event row still reports 99.3% coverage but only 85.7% cost accuracy. (source blocks: T0014)
- At trace availability 0.4 and event availability 0.50, increasing the confidence floor from 0.80 to 0.95 changes reported coverage from 99.6% to 84.4%, pooled spend from 0.4% to 15.6%, and misattributed spend from 7.57% to 0.00%; the resulting 100.0% reported accuracy is an observed synthetic outcome, not a proof of general accuracy. (source blocks: T0014, H0138)
- For E2's one synthetic month, the authors report unmetered-spend recovery within 0.24 percentage points of the bill; the largest displayed error is 0.23 points, with 6.56% planted versus 6.33% recovered, and overbilling flagged at $18.24 versus $18.24 planted, while the corresponding underbilling residual is $-3.50 versus $-3.18 planted rather than exact recovery. (source blocks: H0143, T0015, C0013)
- On E3's 20,000 synthetic test transactions, 5% designated runaway, threshold 0.5 reduces reported p99 incurred cost from $0.701 ungated to $0.276, approximately 61%, with 9.3% reported spend saved, a 4.12% reported false-positive rate, and 74.4% of runaways caught. (source blocks: H0149, T0016)
- At threshold 0.9, E3 reports p99 incurred cost of $0.358, approximately 49% below ungated, 6.2% spend saved, a 1.97% false-positive rate, and 72.0% of runaways caught; threshold 0.95 has identical displayed outcomes, which the author attributes to coarse forecast bins. (source blocks: T0016, H0149, C0015)

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The author explicitly presents an architectural argument and controlled synthetic study, not production validation; chosen collision, feed-availability, retry, runaway, and rounding parameters do not establish real-estate prevalence or absolute financial impact. (source blocks: H0136, H0132, H0172, H0154, H0121)
- The author identifies workload-dependent correlation quality and evolving FOCUS/OpenTelemetry mappings as limitations, and leaves multi-close field validation, L4 benchmarking, and treatment of inference-adjacent overhead to future work. (source blocks: H0132, H0172)
- Reviewer-assessed uncertainty remains around statistical stability: the paper specifies a fixed seed and presents point estimates without confidence intervals or multi-seed variability; its linked code and synthetic artifacts are unverified in this review, not established to be unavailable. (source blocks: H0136, H0154)
- The signed-lineage and delegated-budget-envelope extension is not established by the reported E1–E3 evaluations; signature verification alone does not demonstrate complete usage capture, and concurrent child-budget allocation is not sufficiently specified to infer a conserved global spending cap. (source blocks: H0089, T0014, H0143, H0149)
- Unresolved accounting and attribution uncertainties remain: Equation 2's full-commitment amortization is not reconciled with separately allocated idle, and an unexplained billing residual does not by itself distinguish unsanctioned usage from missing sanctioned telemetry or unresolved billing effects. (source blocks: H0103, H0119, H0143, T0015)
- The architecture's exclusion of prompt content is a useful minimization choice, but the paper does not establish privacy guarantees for identities and linked business metadata; its market mapping is illustrative, contains an unresolved component-count inconsistency, and does not empirically substantiate deployment-time estimates. (source blocks: H0076, H0162, T0017)

## Critical interpretation

- The strongest contribution is the clear separation of settlement truth, runtime transaction assignment, and provenance-bearing reconciliation; however, the qualitative claim that all requirements are met should be read as design coverage, not an operational demonstration against implemented competing systems. (source blocks: H0058, H0080, H0097, C0003, H0131)
- Coverage must not be used as a proxy for correct attribution: the study itself shows substantial wrong-transaction assignment despite near-complete cost coverage, and its claim of 'provable 100% accuracy' exceeds what an observed zero-error synthetic setting can establish. (source blocks: T0014, H0138)
- The planted-effect experiment supports feasibility of residual decomposition under its observable billing assumptions, but not uniquely identifying shadow AI in deployment; missing sanctioned records and interacting billing discrepancies are alternative explanations that require independent evidence, and Table IX already shows imperfect omission recovery. (source blocks: H0074, H0119, H0143, T0015)
- E3 measures spending after trajectories are paused, so reduced incurred cost can reflect deferred or unfinished work rather than improved cost per completed outcome; completion rates, resumed-work charges, approval labor, delay, and quality are needed before interpreting the reported savings as business efficiency. (source blocks: H0149, T0016)
- Commitment accounting needs a conserved allocation identity: distributing the entire commitment cost through Equation 2 and then adding idle as described risks double counting unless the numerator or allocation policy is explicitly partitioned; the source does not supply that reconciliation. (source blocks: H0101, H0103, H0119)
- The realization section should separate an illustrative component mapping from evidence of market absence or implementation speed: its 'seven of eight' availability statement coexists with two unavailable standalone components, and the stated weeks-to-value estimate is not a measured result. (source blocks: H0162, T0017)
